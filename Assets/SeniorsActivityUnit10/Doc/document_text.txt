There is no situation, no decision and no place. It is a list of daily habits and a page and a half of life advice.You cannot teach this in twenty minutes"Sleep for eight hours." "Drink eight glasses of water." "Read more books."None of that can be taught in a lesson and none of it can be played. A child does not learn to sleep well by tapping a button. The content only means anything if it is returned to across weeks.So Unit 10 is not something a class completes. It is a board that stays open all term, gets two minutes a week, and slowly fills in.It also sits outside the Seven Doors on the home screen, as tile 4, for exactly this reason. The seven doors are games you finish. A plant that has been going well is taller and has flowers; one that has been forgotten is smaller but never dead, never brown and never sad-looking.No plant ever diesThis matters. A wilting or dying plant would make a class of eight-year-olds feel they have failed, and several of them would take it personally.A neglected plant simply stays small and green, waiting. When the class does better the following week, it grows again. There is no punishment state anywhere in this unit.3.  The Weekly Check — Two MinutesOnce a week, at the start or end of any lesson. The teacher taps WEEKLY CHECK and it takes two minutes.StepWhat happensTime1The board shows one habit at a time, with its picture. "Who drank their water most days this week?"15 s each2Hands go up. The teacher taps the plus button a few times — roughly, not exactly. Nobody counts precisely.—3The plant grows by an amount based on how many hands went up.5 s4After all four, the garden is shown as a whole, a little fuller than last week.10 s5The Golden Line of the week appears. The teacher reads it and talks for a minute.60 sRoughly, not exactlyThe teacher is estimating a show of hands, not counting thirty children. Three taps for a few hands, ten taps for most of the class.The plant should grow visibly for any number of hands above zero. A class that did badly still sees something happen, because a week where six children remembered is still a week where six children remembered.3.1  No individual tracking, everThe app never asks for a name and never stores anything about a single child.No child is ever asked to say in front of the class that they did not do something.Hands are counted as a group. A child who puts their hand up when they should not have is not a problem worth solving.Several of these habits — sleeping early, drinking water, spending time with family — are not fully within a child’s control. The garden measures the class, not the child, precisely so that nobody is held responsible for their home.That last point is the most important line in this document. Do not build individual tracking into this unit, however often it is asked for.4.  The Golden Line Of The WeekOne line from pages 39 and 40, shown large, once a week, after the garden check. This is the one part of Unit 10 that happens every day rather than every week, and it takes about ten seconds.A glass jar sits in the corner of the garden screen. Any time a child does something kind — or notices somebody else doing something kind — the teacher taps the jar and a small golden marble drops in with a soft chime.RuleDetailAnybody can earn oneIncluding a child reporting somebody else’s kindness, which is the better versionNo namesThe board never records who. The jar is the class’s, not anyone’s.No limitA good day can put six marbles in the jar.It never emptiesMarbles stay for the whole term.It fills slowlySized so that a normal class fills it at around week eleven, not week three.The jar is the only daily thing in the productEverything else in this app happens in a lesson. The jar sits on the board and can be tapped at any moment of any day, which is what makes Unit 10 feel present rather than finished.It also gives the teacher something to do with a good moment. A child helps another child pick up their books, and ten seconds later there is a chime and a marble, and the whole class looks up.6. What matters is that a child at the back of the room can see that a plant is taller this week than it was last week, so the difference between stages must be obvious rather than subtle.8.3  PersistenceThe garden must survive the app being closed, the device being restarted, and the whole thing being updated. Losing a class’s garden in week nine would be the single worst failure in this product.Store it per class code, locally, and back it up whenever there is a connection.The teacher panel needs a way to view the garden without changing it, and a well-guarded reset for the start of a new term.8.4  Build orderBuild the jar first. It is ten minutes of work, it can be used from day one, and it is the part teachers will use most.Build the garden and the weekly check next, with two habits rather than eight, and run it with a real class for three weeks before drawing the other six.The twelve golden lines need no engineering at all and are simply twelve statement screens.The certificate can be built last, at any point before the end of the first term the app is used.8.5  What to watch in the first termObservationWhat it meansThe teacher taps the jar without being remindedThe best possible sign.
